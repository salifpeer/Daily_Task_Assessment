<h3>Sequence-to-Sequence Models, Transfer Learning, and LLMs</h3>

<p>
Today I learned about <b>Sequence-to-Sequence Models</b>, Encoder-Decoder architecture, Attention mechanisms, Transformers, Transfer Learning, and the working of Large Language Models (LLMs), including ChatGPT.
</p>

<br>

<h3>1. Sequence-to-Sequence Models</h3>

<p>
Learned how sequence-to-sequence models transform an input sequence into an output sequence, commonly used in machine translation and text summarization.
</p>

<h3>2. Encoder-Decoder Model</h3>

<p>
Learned how the encoder processes input data and the decoder generates the output sequence based on the encoded information.
</p>

<h3>3. Attention Model</h3>

<p>
Studied how attention mechanisms help models focus on the most relevant parts of the input when generating outputs.
</p>

<h3>4. Transformer Model</h3>

<p>
Learned how Transformers use self-attention and other neural network components to process sequences efficiently and understand relationships between tokens.
</p>

<h3>5. Transfer Learning</h3>

<p>
Learned how a pre-trained model can be adapted to a new task using existing learned knowledge, reducing the need to train a model from scratch.
</p>

<h3>6. Working of LLMs and ChatGPT</h3>

<p>
Learned how Large Language Models (LLMs) process text as tokens and generate responses by predicting the next token based on the given context. Also studied the role of pre-training, fine-tuning, and context windows in generating responses.
</p>

<p>
Understood the basic working of ChatGPT, including how it processes prompts, uses learned patterns to generate responses, and produces text one token at a time.
</p>

<h3>Conclusion</h3>

<p>
Gained a basic understanding of sequence-to-sequence architectures, attention, Transformers, Transfer Learning, and how LLMs such as ChatGPT generate responses.
</p>
